Field manual / fundamentals

Movement & aim

Treat movement and aim as one system: arrive with the crosshair ready, take the shot, then make the return shot difficult.

Move with a reason

Constant motion is not automatically safe. Move to change what an opponent must predict: your timing, direction, distance, or height. In a one-bullet duel, a short unpredictable reset can matter more than a long sprint in the open.

ANGLE

Leave the firing line

After shooting, step away from the exact line where the opponent last saw you.

COVER

Keep an exit nearby

Choose positions that let you hide during the vulnerable part of the cycle.

RHYTHM

Vary your timing

A repeated peek becomes easy to pre-aim. Pause, change side, or take another route.

AWARENESS

Scan beyond the duel

Free-for-all pressure can arrive from any direction, even after a successful shot.

Make the shot smaller

Good crosshair placement reduces how far you must correct when a target appears. Aim where a player is likely to enter the screen, then make the final adjustment with control instead of a panic flick.

  • Pre-aim common entrances. Let map geometry narrow the possible target locations.
  • Track before firing. A fraction of confirmation can be safer than an immediate miss.
  • Keep input settings stable. Change one setting at a time and judge it over several matches.
  • Practice the reset. Your plan after the shot should be decided before you fire.

A simple practice routine

  1. Play one match focused only on crosshair placement.
  2. Play one match focused only on reaching cover after each shot.
  3. Combine both: pre-aim, fire, then leave the angle immediately.
  4. Review patterns, not single outcomes. One lucky hit does not prove a habit is strong.
NO SECRET SETTINGS

There is no universal sensitivity or device setup. Use values you can control consistently, and treat any community recommendation as personal preference.
